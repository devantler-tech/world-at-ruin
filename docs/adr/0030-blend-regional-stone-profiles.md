# Blend regional exposed-stone profiles

Status: Accepted for the default-off preview

## Context

The Reach has deterministic regional palettes and landforms, but exposed stone
still uses one global ash threshold and slope tendency. Issue #550 asks regions
to decide where stone remains buried or scoured without replacing the stable
slab field, introducing boundary seams or changing ordinary launches.

## Decision

Each GroundRegions entry declares `stone_density` and `stone_scour`. These map
to the drift threshold and rock-slope threshold respectively; density is a
coverage tendency, not a promised percentage of the region's area. The existing
nine region sites and nine-metre blend band supply normalized, maximum-per-region
weights. Duplicate sites of one region do not multiply its influence, and all
competing regions participate at triple junctions.

One CPU accessor returns the blended pair. Geometry uses it at the slab site,
corners and outward ash samples, keeping tops, collision and edge rubble tied
to the same decision. Slab identities, cells, substance hashes and lip thickness
remain independent of region ownership. There is one overlay, not one mesh per
region. Continuous profile weights do not claim continuous geometry topology:
the field still makes deterministic exposure decisions.

Both terrain shaders include one shared GPU blend implementation. The world
passes the same sites, profiles and blend band to terrain and cave contact
materials. The cave retains both live preview gates when its material is rebuilt.
The existing global shader arithmetic and CPU defaults remain the opt-out path.

Only exact `WAR_REGION_STONE=1` opts into the new profile, and rendered stone
also requires the existing `WAR_GROUND_PLATES=1`. Evidence tools can choose the
regional profile through the existing live plate setter. Changing that profile
rebuilds the one overlay and collision; switching off restores the original
field and foliage inventory. No save or protocol format changes.

## Consequences

Tests hold decided interiors, two- and three-way blends, duplicate-site
invariance, stable slab identity, materially different normalized real-world
coverage, fresh/live convergence, cave rebuilds and both opt-out paths. Existing
palette and terrain goldens remain unchanged, and the existing plate regressions
pass without fixture changes. A native six-point shader
probe checks CPU/GPU profile transport within one RGB8 quantum; it does not
certify complete exposure cutline parity or a GPU frame-time budget.

Fixed near-field captures compare the neutral and regional profiles with
unchanged repeat, restore and plates-off controls in two regions. The preview
still needs convincing edge wear, contact shadows and less tiled shapes, plus
the existing raised-stone hardware activation gate. Issue #1287 owns the
activation or rejection decision and flag retirement, with review due 2026-11-07.
